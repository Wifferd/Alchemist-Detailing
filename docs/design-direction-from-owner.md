Absolutely. The website should feel less like a normal local car-wash website and more like a premium automotive brand. The reference points we’ve established are Nike-level presentation + Aston Martin-style motion + Alchemist’s black/gold identity.

The most important thing is that it should feel expensive without becoming complicated.

Alchemist Detailing — Website Design Direction

1. Overall visual identity

Main colors

Primary

* Deep black
* Charcoal
* Very dark grey

Accent

* Rich metallic gold
* Warm champagne gold for highlights

Secondary

* Off-white
* Warm grey

Gold should not cover everything.

It should be used strategically for:

* Buttons
* Active navigation
* Selected options
* Important numbers
* Borders
* Glows
* Animations
* Logos
* Small highlights

The site should be approximately:

70–80% black/charcoal
15–20% off-white/grey
5–10% gold

That keeps the gold special.

⸻

2. The top navigation

This is one of the most important parts.

On desktop and mobile, there should be a sleek floating/translucent bar near the top.

Something like:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [☰]                  HOME                    [AD] [◎] │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

But not literally a rectangular box with hard edges.

It should feel like a piece of dark glass floating above the page.

Left

A menu button.

The button sits inside the navigation bar but has its own subtle bevel/raised appearance.

When pressed:

* It slightly depresses
* Gold glow appears
* Menu slides in

Center

The current page name:

HOME

If you’re on Services:

SERVICES

If you’re booking:

BOOK

etc.

Right

The Alchemist AD logo followed by the account icon.

The logo should remain relatively small.

Don’t make the navigation look like a giant corporate header.

36. The most important design principle

I would tell Claude this:

Do not make Alchemist look like a car wash website. Make it look like a premium automotive brand that happens to provide detailing services.

That’s the difference.

Nike doesn’t make you feel like you’re looking at a shoe catalog.

Aston Martin doesn’t make you feel like you’re looking at a dealership database.

Alchemist should make someone feel the quality before they even understand the services.

Then when they click Book Now, the flashy experience becomes a clean, extremely easy booking application.

That contrast is what I’d aim for:

Homepage: cinematic + emotional
Services: premium + visual
Booking: simple + precise
Account: clean + personal
Admin: fast + functional
Team: minimal + operational.
