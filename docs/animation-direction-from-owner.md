Yes — what you’re describing is called scroll-driven animation or scroll-scrubbing. Instead of scrolling simply moving the page upward, the user’s scroll position controls an animation.

For Alchemist, I would make this one of the website’s signature features.

1. The foam-cannon transition

Imagine the customer is on the homepage.

They see:

SERVICES

with a beautiful vehicle in the background.

As they begin scrolling:

1. The page slows down.
2. A detailing foam cannon enters from the side.
3. It begins spraying foam toward the screen.
4. The foam starts accumulating around the edges.
5. As they continue scrolling, more of the screen becomes covered.
6. The screen becomes almost completely covered in white/cream foam.
7. The foam slowly slides/drips away.
8. The Services section is revealed underneath.
9. The page releases the scroll and continues normally.

So the customer essentially scrolls through the washing process.

The important part

Don’t make this a normal animation that simply plays after someone scrolls.

Make the scroll position control the animation.

For example:

Scroll 0%
    ↓
Cannon appears
Scroll 25%
    ↓
Foam begins spraying
Scroll 50%
    ↓
Screen increasingly covered
Scroll 75%
    ↓
Almost completely covered
Scroll 100%
    ↓
Foam clears → SERVICES revealed

That gives the customer the feeling that they are controlling the wash with their scroll.

⸻

2. Make the foam feel physical

This is where it could become really impressive.

Don’t just put a white PNG over the screen.

The foam should have:

* Large bubbles
* Small bubbles
* Uneven edges
* Slight transparency
* Glossy highlights
* Dripping
* Different thicknesses
* Small bubbles floating upward
* Subtle movement

The edges should look like actual foam touching a camera lens.

And because the Alchemist palette is black/gold, you could have tiny warm/gold reflections inside some bubbles.

Very subtle.

⸻

3. The cannon itself

The cannon shouldn’t necessarily dominate the screen.

It could briefly appear:

from the bottom-right or left side

and spray diagonally across the viewer’s screen.

Something like:

                   FOAM
              ☁☁☁☁☁☁☁
          ↗↗↗↗↗↗↗↗↗
       [FOAM CANNON]

Then it disappears once the transition is complete.

That keeps it cinematic rather than gimmicky.

⸻

The water could distort the background slightly as it moves.

That would be a signature Alchemist transition.

20. The “gold shimmer” system

I’d actually create one consistent animation system for the entire website.

Whenever something important happens:

Gold light moves across it.

For example:

BOOK NOW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
    → GOLD LIGHT →
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Then the same effect appears on:

* Active navigation
* Selected services
* Featured review
* Important headings
* Completed appointments
* Primary CTA

That makes the site feel designed as one system rather than a bunch of unrelated animations.

⸻

21. One thing I would NOT do

Don’t make every section crazy.

If the homepage has:

* Foam cannon
* Water
* Gold particles
* Spinning cars
* Flying buttons
* Parallax
* Video
* Text bouncing
* Cards rotating

everywhere, it will look like a flashy template.

Instead:

One major animation per major section.

For example:

Hero → cinematic vehicle

Services → foam cannon

Exterior → water/paint

Protection → water beading

Interior → cabin transition

Reviews → gold reveal

About → cinematic story

Booking → smooth application transition

That’s enough.
